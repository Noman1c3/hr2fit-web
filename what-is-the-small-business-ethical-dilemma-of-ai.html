AI & Ethics

What Is the Small Business Ethical Dilemma of AI?

Dr. Cindy Cushman Founder & Owner, HR2fit • July 5, 2026 • 6 min read

To answer this question, we need to understand what constitutes an ethical dilemma. An ethical dilemma is a complex situation in which a person must choose among two or more conflicting moral principles, with no clear right or wrong option. The key feature of an ethical dilemma is that any decision made will violate some ethical norm, creating moral tension and uncertainty.

Ethical dilemmas (also called moral dilemmas) occur when an individual, SMB, or group faces conflicting moral obligations or values and must decide which to prioritize. Unlike straightforward right-versus-wrong choices, ethical dilemmas involve conflicts between two right options.

In small businesses, owners often face conflicting duties or values when making AI-related decisions, constituting an ethical dilemma. Multiple ethical principles may apply, but following one can violate another. For example, in a small business, customer service is crucial. Many small businesses are known for their customer service models. To cut costs and stay competitive, an SMB owner might decide to add AI to its customer service decision process.

This decision could eliminate jobs or make clients and customers feel they are not receiving the customer service they need or want as the human element has been removed. The upside includes cost savings and a competitive edge, while the downside is that the SMB could lose personal relationships with clients and customers, and it may impact employment. This presents an ethical dilemma for many SMB owners.

Privacy Loss & Data Ethics

Consider the issue of privacy loss: surveillance tools and data-mining algorithms can gather and analyze personal information in ways that infringe upon privacy or consent. SMB owners must be aware of AI-driven surveillance tools that could potentially breach customer agreements or employ data-mining technologies without user consent.

How to Solve the Small Business Ethical Dilemma of AI?

To address the Small Business Ethical Dilemma related to AI, it is essential to determine whether an ethical dilemma truly exists. SMB owners should ask themselves if the situation involves a moral or ethical conflict. What moral principles are in conflict? In essence, an SMB owner's dilemma arises when every available choice violates some moral value or leads to harm that the owner did not anticipate.

The SMB owner needs to assess their needs and decide if AI can effectively solve their challenges. It is crucial to have a clear understanding of the specific issues they face and to consider whether AI is genuinely the best solution or if they are just trying to jump on the AI bandwagon.

1. The Utilitarian Framework

"The needs of the many outweigh the needs of the one." — Star Trek

This approach can be considered Utilitarianism—an ethical theory that judges an action's morality based on its consequences, specifically how much overall happiness or well-being it produces. The central idea is to maximize utility, meaning to create the greatest good for the greatest number of people.

An SMB owner in the food manufacturing industry decided to implement an AI customer service system to handle common inquiries from customers. The owner chose this AI solution to improve response times, reduce costs, and maintain high-quality, consistent service without needing to hire additional staff. By introducing an AI product that enhances efficiency, the business can achieve cost and time savings not only for the owner but also for the entire staff, benefiting the business as a whole.

2. The Deontological Framework (Duty & Rules)

"Do the right thing because it is right." — Immanuel Kant

Deontological ethics emphasizes the importance of intentions and moral principles over the consequences of actions. Its primary focus is on duty and strict adherence to rules rather than the results of those actions.

Consider an engineering firm that implemented an AI product to predict project timelines. Profits surged, but the owner discovered the tool had algorithmic flaws affecting deliverables. A deontological approach dictated full disclosure to clients to maintain integrity and human dignity, rather than concealing the error to preserve short-term profits.

3. Virtue Ethics (Character & Balance)

"We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act but a habit." — Aristotle

Virtue Ethics emphasizes a person's character rather than adhering to rigid rules or focusing solely on outcomes. Instead of asking "What should I do?", Virtue Ethics poses the question "What kind of person should I be?"

A key concept is the "Golden Mean"—finding a balanced middle ground between extremes. When evaluating AI tools, business owners must ensure that automation decisions reflect prudence, fairness, and transparency, sustaining the enterprise without sacrificing long-term client trust.
