What Is the Small Business Ethical Dilemma of AI?

Dr. Cindy Cushman Founder & Owner, HR2fit • July 05, 2026 • 12 min read

To answer this question, we need to understand what constitutes an ethical dilemma. An ethical dilemma is a complex situation in which a person must choose among two or more conflicting moral principles, with no clear right or wrong option. The key feature of an ethical dilemma is that any decision made will violate some ethical norm, creating moral tension and uncertainty. Ethical dilemmas (also called moral dilemmas) occur when an individual, SMB, or group faces conflicting moral obligations or values and must decide which to prioritize. Unlike straightforward right-versus-wrong choices, ethical dilemmas involve conflicts between two right options.

In small businesses, owners often face conflicting duties or values when making AI-related decisions, constituting an ethical dilemma. Multiple ethical principles may apply, but following one can violate another. For example, in a small business, customer service is crucial. Many small businesses are known for their customer service models. To cut costs and stay competitive, an SMB owner might decide to add AI to its customer service decision process. This decision could eliminate jobs or make clients and customers feel they are not receiving the customer service they need or want as the human element has been removed. The upside includes cost savings and a competitive edge, while the downside is that the SMB could lose personal relationships with clients and customers, and it may impact employment. This presents an ethical dilemma for many SMB owners.

In certain situations, there may not be a clear solution, as every available option has negative moral consequences. A negative moral consequence refers to the harmful outcome that results from actions that violate moral or ethical principles. In simpler terms, it is the emotional, social, or spiritual fallout that occurs when someone engages in behavior deemed wrong or unethical.

For instance, consider the issue of privacy loss: surveillance tools and data-mining algorithms can gather and analyze personal information in ways that infringe upon privacy or consent. SMB owners must be aware of AI-driven surveillance tools that could potentially breach their customer agreements or employ data-mining technologies without user consent. Using AI in customer service decision-making may invade the privacy of an SMB&#8217;s clients and customers if the data-mining tool is unknown to them. When such infringements are discovered, the SMB owner may experience negative moral consequences because the AI services used compromised their customers&#8217; privacy without consent.

Many SMB owners see AI as having a positive impact on their businesses, including increased efficiency, better customer insights to enhance marketing strategies, and improved decision-making through more detailed information and understanding. Conversely, an SMB owner can face significant consequences if they implement the wrong AI in their workplace. SMB business owners must weigh the benefits, risks, and ethical consequences of their AI-related decisions. This process is similar to the yin-yang concept, in which complementary forces work together to create balance and harmony. Although the stakes are high when it comes to integrating AI, each decision involves ethical considerations. SMB owners&#8217; choices can impact people, relationships, or their company&#8217;s integrity.

To address the Small Business Ethical Dilemma related to AI, it is essential to determine whether an ethical dilemma truly exists. SMB owners should ask themselves if the situation involves a moral or ethical conflict. What moral principles are in conflict? In essence, an SMB owner&#8217;s dilemma arises when every available choice violates some moral value or leads to harm that the owner did not anticipate. Therefore, reasoning and judgment—rather than merely following rules—are necessary for making the decision. When evaluating which course of action to take, SMB owners should consider both the short-term and long-term outcomes of each option, including emotional, social, and reputational effects. The goal is to identify the option that causes the least harm or generates the most good.

The SMB owner needs to assess their needs and decide if AI can effectively solve their challenges. It is crucial to have a clear understanding of the specific issues they face and to consider whether AI is genuinely the best solution or if they are just trying to jump on the AI bandwagon. Embedded in this decision-making process are the ethical considerations that have come up. The risks and ethical implications of using AI should be well understood during this evaluation. Additionally, it is important to think about whether using AI will not only address the current challenge but also improve the company&#8217;s efficiency, accuracy, and speed in the future. Forecasting whether AI will ultimately help or hurt the company in multiple ways, including the business owner&#8217;s moral compass, is essential in the decision-making process.

Ultimately, the SMB owner should choose the course of action they believe is most ethically justifiable and be prepared to explain their reasoning openly. There are three common frameworks for resolving ethical dilemmas that may help the SMB determine the best path forward and a way to communicate their decision.

This can best be described by the famous Spock quote from Star Trek

&#8220;The needs of the many outweigh the needs of the one.&#8221;

This approach can be considered Utilitarianism—an ethical theory that judges an action&#8217;s morality based on its consequences, specifically how much overall happiness or well-being it produces. The central idea is to maximize utility, meaning to create the greatest good for the greatest number of people.

Although SMBs are often recognized for their customer service, which tends to focus on one-on-one relationships, the Utilitarianism framework can still apply to them. The emphasis is on the company as a whole and on the SMB owner&#8217;s willingness to prioritize the needs of all stakeholders, embodying the idea of the &#8220;greatest good for the greatest number.&#8221; This framework is relevant for SMBs, especially when they are considering large-scale changes, such as implementing AI across the company. Communication is key to the success of any AI initiative in this context.

An SMB owner in the food manufacturing industry decided to implement an AI customer service system to handle common inquiries from customers. The owner chose this AI solution to improve response times, reduce costs, and maintain high-quality, consistent service without needing to hire additional staff. However, during the transition, there was some pushback from two employees and one of the company&#8217;s most important customers. This situation created an ethical dilemma for the business owner. Using the Utilitarianism model, the owner ultimately decided to proceed with the new service, even at the risk of losing a valuable customer and causing employee dissatisfaction. The owner believed that adopting the AI customer service product would generate the most benefit for the company overall.

By introducing an AI product that enhances efficiency, the business can achieve cost and time savings not only for the owner but also for the entire staff. Additionally, it provides a new option for customers and clients. This aligns with a utilitarian approach, considering the needs of the many stakeholders involved.

Utilitarianism is an ethical theory that judges actions by their consequences, aiming to create the greatest good for the greatest number. This framework can guide SMBs in making decisions that affect multiple stakeholders. Although SMBs often focus on personalized customer service, utilitarianism encourages considering the broader impact on employees, customers, and the business as a whole. As in the example above, adopting AI tools to handle customer inquiries can improve efficiency, reduce costs, and free up staff time while maintaining quality service. Such changes benefit multiple groups simultaneously, reflecting the utilitarian goal of maximizing overall well-being.

This can best be described by the creator of deontological ethics, Immanuel Kant:

&#8220;Do the right thing because it is right.&#8221;

Deontological ethics emphasizes the importance of intentions and moral principles over the consequences of actions. Its primary focus is on duty and strict adherence to rules rather than the results of those actions. Immanuel Kant, a key figure in this ethical framework, argued that an action is moral if it conforms to a moral rule or principle, regardless of its consequences. His philosophy is based on two main ideas: the Universal Law, which states that one should act only according to maxims that could be embraced as universal laws (essentially asking, &#8220;If everyone acted this way, would it still be acceptable?&#8221;), and Human Dignity, which mandates that every person be treated as an end in themselves and never merely as a means to achieve personal objectives.

An SMB owner of an engineering firm implemented an AI product that helps predict project timelines. Everything was going well, and profits were significantly up for the engineering firm; bonuses were being planned. Then a discovery was made: the AI product that had brought them great success was not implemented properly, and all the projects that used it were flawed. This created an ethical dilemma for the SMB owner. The owner could choose to remain silent and not inform clients, blaming significant project delays on other factors, or disclose the implementation error, risking damage to their reputation and future profits. Adopting a deontological approach, the business owner decided to disclose the error and work towards regaining their clients&#8217; trust.

Deontology, developed by Immanuel Kant, emphasizes the importance of doing what is morally right based on rules and duties rather than focusing on the outcomes of actions. It highlights the need to act with good intentions and adhere to principles that could apply universally, while also respecting the inherent dignity of every person. In practice, this means choosing honesty and integrity, even if it results in negative consequences. For example, a business owner might admit to an AI-related mistake to clients, even if this admission could lead to financial loss.

This can best be described by Aristotle&#8217;s philosophy

&#8220;We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act but a habit.&#8221;

Virtue Ethics is an ethical approach primarily associated with Aristotle. It emphasizes a person&#8217;s character rather than adhering to specific rules (as in deontology) or focusing solely on outcomes (as in utilitarianism). Instead of asking, &#8220;What should I do?&#8221; Virtue Ethics poses the question, &#8220;What kind of person should I be?&#8221; The underlying idea is that moral behavior stems from cultivating good character traits, known as virtues, such as honesty, courage, fairness, and integrity. When someone consistently practices these virtues, they are more likely to make ethical decisions. A key concept in Virtue Ethics is the &#8220;Golden Mean,&#8221; which involves finding a balanced middle ground between extremes. For instance, courage represents a balance between recklessness and cowardice.

An SMB owner of a sports memorabilia store has established a strong online presence, a physical storefront, and a loyal following at trade shows. The owner is passionate about the storefront, as many longtime customers visit frequently. However, like many brick-and-mortar stores, the business&#8217;s operating costs have increased, and customer traffic has decreased, especially since implementing AI services in the online store. The owner faced an ethical dilemma: while the storefront still generated profit, it was draining resources away from the more successful online operations, which had significantly increased sales due to AI services. After careful consideration, the owner decided to close the storefront to keep the company solvent. This decision meant laying off the staff who worked in the store and saying goodbye to the long-time customers who visited regularly. Taking virtue ethics into account, the owner&#8217;s decision was based on the need to ensure the company&#8217;s viability, even though it caused significant inconvenience to current customers and was emotionally challenging for the SMB owner.

Virtue Ethics, based on Aristotle&#8217;s philosophy, focuses on developing good character traits—like honesty, courage, and integrity—rather than following rules or focusing on outcomes. It asks, &#8220;What kind of person should I be?&#8221; and teaches that ethical behavior comes from consistently practicing virtues and finding balance (the &#8220;Golden Mean&#8221;). In business, this means making decisions that reflect strong character, such as a business owner closing a struggling storefront to responsibly sustain the company, even when the choice is difficult.

The Small Business Ethical Dilemma of AI occurs when SMB owners must choose between conflicting moral values—such as cost efficiency vs. employee impact, or innovation vs. customer privacy—with no clear &#8220;right&#8221; answer. AI can improve efficiency, decision-making, and competitiveness, but it can also lead to job loss, reduced personal relationships, and privacy concerns.

To navigate these dilemmas, business owners must carefully weigh risks, benefits, and ethical consequences, considering both short- and long-term impacts. They should ensure AI is truly needed, understand its implications, and choose the most ethically justifiable option.

Ultimately, SMB owners must balance business success with ethical responsibility and be ready to justify their decisions.
